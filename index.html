Which Difference-in-Differences
Estimator Should I Use?

There is no single best Difference-in-Differences estimator. The right estimator depends on the treatment path and the effect you want to estimate. Is treatment binary or nonbinary? Absorbing or reversible? Do groups adopt at different dates? Can treatment change repeatedly? Are dynamic effects plausible? Are there untreated or not-yet-treated groups, stayers, or quasi-untreated observations? This guide first diagnoses the design, then explains which modern DiD estimators are compatible with it and what assumptions each requires.

Stata users can also run whichDIDshouldIuse for an automatic description of their treatment design — see below.

Every design in this guide sits somewhere on these dimensions: treatment support (binary or nonbinary), absorbing or reversible, common or staggered timing, repeated switches, lagged/dynamic effects, and the controls or stayers available for comparison. The rows below map each combination onto its chapter, the compatible packages, and what it costs you in assumptions.

  • Never-, not-yet-treated units or stayers available
  • Simultaneous heterogeneous adoption (HAD)
  • Target estimand: static effect, dose-response, or event-study path

Diagnose your own design

    
Five criteria. The first (a usable comparison group) is a prerequisite; the other four can be answered in any order and combine to determine the diagnosis. The panel below updates as you answer, and once it lands on a design it links straight to the matching row in the comparison table above.


    


      

        
        Current diagnosis
        Answer any question to begin
        
Comparison group, treatment type, and timing together place you in a design; dynamics and stayers refine it further.

        

        

      


      
Criteria — 01 is a prerequisite; 02–05 combine to place the design.


      

        
01 · Controls / stayers

        
Do you have never-treated units, not-yet-treated units, or stayers to compare against?

        

          
          
        

      


      

        
02 · Treatment type

        
Is treatment binary, and does it stay on once switched (absorbing) — or is it nonbinary (discrete/continuous), or can it switch off?

        

          
          
          
        

      


      

        
03 · Timing

        
Do all treated units start on the same calendar date? For a nonbinary treatment, "common date" means everyone starts untreated and is dosed together — heterogeneous adoption (HAD).

        

          
          
        

      


      

        
04 · Repeated switches / lagged effects

        
Can a unit's treatment change more than once, or should past treatment still affect the outcome?

        

          
          
        

      


      

        
05 · Switchers & stayers / target estimand

        
Comparable switchers and stayers, and a static (not dynamic) target?
