Which Difference-in-Differences
Estimator Should I Use?

There is no universally best Difference-in-Differences estimator. The right estimator depends on the design of your study and the effect you want to estimate.

Is treatment binary or nonbinary? Absorbing, meaning that once treated one always remains treated, or reversible? Do treated groups adopt at different dates? Can treatment change more than once? Is it plausible that lagged treatments still affect the current outcome? Are there untreated or not-yet-treated groups, stayers, or quasi-untreated observations? This guide first helps you characterize your research design, and then explains which modern DiD estimators are compatible with it.

Stata users can also run whichDIDshouldIuse for an automatic characterization of their design — see below.

            Heterogeneous adoption design
            All units are untreated at the start of the panel, all treated units become treated at the same date, and treated units receive heterogeneous treatment doses.
            
              
            
            
              If there are never-treated units
              
  • A DID estimator using the never-treated as controls, scaled by the average dose received by treated units

              Without untreated units, with some quasi-untreated units
              
  • A DID estimator using the quasi-untreated as controls, scaled by the average dose received by treated units

              Without untreated or quasi-untreated units
              
  • A DID estimator using the least-treated as controls, scaled by the average dose received by treated units. NB: without untreated or quasi-untreated units, this estimator relies on a homogeneous effect assumption.

Identify your design automatically in Stata

    
Run whichDIDshouldIuse on your data for an automatic characterization of your design and a menu of estimators you can use given your design.

    

      
This diagnostic helps classify your design; it is not a substitute for assessing the identifying assumptions underlying each estimator.

      

        

          Stata
          ssc install whichDIDshouldIuse
          
        

        

          Stata
          ssc install distinct
          
        

        

          Stata
          whichDIDshouldIuse Y G T D
          
        

      

      
Requires distinct (SSC). Not yet on SSC for your version? Check the GitHub repository.
